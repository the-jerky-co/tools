Read this once Competition terms are a different animal to sale terms. Australian trade promotions can carry state permit requirements depending on prize value and whether the promotion is a game of chance (random draw) or a game of skill (judged). Those rules vary by state and have changed in recent years — verify current requirements before publishing, and get a legal check on anything with a high-value prize. This tool produces a starting draft, not legal advice.

Why the wording changed Across the 11 promos sent between May and July 2026, the same six ideas were written five different ways — "Can not be combined" vs "Cannot be combined", a timezone stated in only five of eleven, and one grammatical error ("Can not to be used") copied across both Winter Warmers sends. The clauses below are the single canonical version of each. Use the tool rather than copying an old email.

House rules

Cannot
One word, always. Never "can not".
Timezone
Always stated. Default AWST — we're a Perth business, but say so or an east-coast customer reads it as their own time.
Date format
11:59pm AWST, Sunday 16 August 2026. Time first, then weekday, then date.
Domain
thejerkyco.com.au — no "www.", no trailing space before the full stop.
Air-dried
Never "dried", "slow-dried" or "dehydrated". Applies in fine print too.
Dollar figures
Fine allowed in thresholds. Brand rule bans dollar breakdowns of the discount itself in body copy — state those as percentages.
Em dash
Not used in fine print. Commas and full stops only. Any em dash typed into a free-text field is swapped for a comma automatically.
Placement
Last text block in the email, below the unsubscribe footer. Centred, 11–12px, grey.

Watch out for

Reminder sends
The "#2" reminder is usually a clone. In July the reminder silently dropped the free-shipping sentence the first send had. Regenerate rather than clone.
Shipping
Two clauses that sound similar mean opposite things — "discount excludes shipping" vs "free shipping over $150". Pick deliberately.
Prizes
Any mention of winning something needs competition terms, even in a NEWS email. The Bulldogs launch promoted a giveaway with none.
9am cutoffs
Non-midnight end times are fine but need the weekday spelled out — 9:00am AWST Monday reads very differently to 11:59pm Sunday.
