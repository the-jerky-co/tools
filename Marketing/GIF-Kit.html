Quick Reference

What makes a GIF heavy

Pixels
Width matters most. Halving the width roughly quarters the file. 480–600px is plenty for email.
Frames
Length × frame rate. 10–15 fps looks smooth for product shots; 5–8 fps for text or slideshow-style clips.
Colours
Fewer colours, smaller file. Flat graphics survive 32–64; footage usually wants 128–256.
Motion
Only changed pixels get stored, so static backgrounds are cheap and handheld camera shake is expensive.
Compression
Ignores near-identical pixel changes between frames. The best size-for-quality trade on real footage.
Dither
Hides colour banding in gradients, but adds noise that costs bytes. Leave off unless banding is obvious.

Good to know

Privacy
Files are processed in your browser tab. Nothing is uploaded or stored.
iPhone .mov
HEVC videos may not play in Chrome on Windows. Export or AirDrop as "Most Compatible" (H.264) if it won't load.
Speed
Long or large clips take a while and use a lot of memory. Trim first; keep it under ~300 frames.
